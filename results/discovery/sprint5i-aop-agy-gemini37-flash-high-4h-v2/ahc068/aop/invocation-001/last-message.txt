### Methodology

The optimization proceeded through five structural milestones:

1. Topological Solvability and Non-Cut Elimination
The grid graph with walls is guaranteed to be connected. Sorting is formulated as sequential leaf elimination: at each step, Tarjan's bridge finding algorithm identifies all non-cut vertices in the remaining unlocked subgraph. Locking a non-cut vertex guarantees that the remaining unlocked cells remain connected, preserving reachability for all remaining cards.

2. State-Space Jump Operations
Instead of single adjacent swaps (1x2 and 2x1), 1D rectangle swaps of height h or width w are used as long jump edges. A horizontal jump across a wall-free corridor of length 2k shifts a card by k cells in 1 operation. BFS on the grid finds the minimum number of jump operations to transport any card to its destination.

3. Peeling Geometry vs Interior Swiss Cheese
Greedy selection of arbitrary nearby non-cut cells creates holes inside the grid, fragmenting long corridors and preventing large jumps in late turns. Establishing strict geometric peeling orders (such as concentric onion rings, diagonal waves, and row/column sweeps) preserves the remaining unlocked cells as solid contiguous blocks, maximizing jump lengths throughout the entire solve.

4. Wall-Aware BFS Tree Elimination Orders
In grids with extensive internal walls, fixed geometric coordinate orders do not match wall topology. Constructing BFS shortest path trees from various root vertices R respecting wall edges provides elimination orders from tree leaves inward toward the root. Farthest vertices are eliminated first, ensuring the remaining component stays connected without artificial detour penalties.

5. Zero-Overhead Tokenized Multi-Root Search and Compression
Using zero-allocation token versioning for Tarjan and BFS searches allows testing over 100 root locations and geometric permutations within the 1.6 second time limit. A post-processing pass commutes disjoint operations and removes redundant pairs to compress final operation sequences.

### Caveats and Technical Notes

- Memory access and array clearing: Re-allocating or clearing 20x20 distance buffers 400 times per solve incurred significant CPU overhead. Replacing buffer zeroing with monotonic token counters reduced per-solve latency by over 60 percent.
- Window selection complexity: Full evaluation of secondary card displacement over all candidate slice offsets proved too computationally expensive and caused a time limit exceeded result in candidate 7. Bounding the window offset to O(1) tight placements maintained low operation counts without timing risks.
